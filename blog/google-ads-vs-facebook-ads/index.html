Google Ads vs Facebook Ads: Which Is Right for Your Small Business?

A plain-language comparison of Google Ads and Facebook & Instagram ads for small businesses: how each works, which fits your business, and how to start without wasting money.

October 22, 2026·7 min read·By Gabriel Macovei

Both Google Ads and Facebook ads can bring new customers to a small business. The difference is when they reach people. Google shows your ad while someone is searching for what you offer. Facebook and Instagram show your ad while someone is scrolling, before they were looking for you at all.

Once you understand that one difference, choosing where to start becomes much easier.

Google Ads: be there when people search

When someone types “emergency plumber near me” or “wedding photographer in Toronto,” Google shows a few ads above the regular results. With Google Ads, your business can be one of them.

  • Best for: services people already know they need—repairs, legal help, clinics, local trades, urgent jobs.
  • How you pay: usually only when someone clicks your ad.
  • What the ads look like: mostly text—a headline, a short description, and links such as your phone number.

Because people are already looking, Google ads often bring visitors who are ready to call or book. The catch is that popular searches can be competitive, so the cost of each click depends on your industry and area.

Facebook & Instagram ads: get noticed while people scroll

Facebook and Instagram ads (both run through Meta’s ad tools) appear in people’s feeds, stories, and reels. You choose the area and the kind of people you want to reach, and your ad appears between the posts they are already looking at.

  • Best for: visual businesses and new offers—salons, restaurants, fitness, events, before-and-after work, promotions, and openings.
  • How you pay: usually by how many people see the ad, or by clicks, depending on the goal you choose.
  • What the ads look like: photos and short videos. Eye-catching visuals matter a lot here.

These ads are great at making people aware of you, even if they were not searching. Short video ads tend to stand out in a busy feed.

A simple way to choose

  • Do people search for what you do? Start with Google.
  • Does your work look great in photos or video? Start with Facebook & Instagram.
  • Is your offer new, seasonal, or a promotion? Facebook & Instagram can create demand.
  • Is it urgent or high-value for the customer? Google usually captures that intent.

Many small businesses do better by doing one platform well before adding a second, rather than splitting a small budget in two.

How much should you spend?

There is no single right number. It depends on your industry, your area, and how much a new customer is worth to you. Many small businesses start testing with around $500 USD a month in ad budget, then adjust once they see which ads bring inquiries. Treat the first months as learning: ads need a few weeks to gather data and improve.

What makes either one work

  • A clear offer. Say exactly what you do, where, and why someone should choose you.
  • A page that is ready for visitors. Send people to a page that matches the ad, loads quickly on phones, and makes it easy to call or book.
  • Tracking. Measure calls, form messages, and bookings, so you know which ads actually bring customers.
  • Fresh creative. On Facebook & Instagram especially, new images and videos keep ads from going stale.

Common mistakes that waste money

  • Sending every ad to your home page instead of a page about that offer
  • Boosting posts without a clear goal
  • Running ads with no way to tell which ones bring customers
  • Giving up after a week, before the ads have had time to improve
  • Splitting a small budget across too many platforms
