How Much Does a Small Business Website Cost in 2026?

What small business websites really cost in 2026, what the price should include, the ongoing costs to budget for, and the questions to ask before you hire anyone.

October 22, 2026·8 min read·By Gabriel Macovei

Ask ten people what a website costs and you will hear ten different answers, from “free” to “tens of thousands of dollars.” Both can be true. The price depends less on the website itself and more on who builds it, how much of the work you do yourself, and what happens after it goes live.

This guide explains the usual options, what a fair price should include, and the ongoing costs many business owners forget to budget for.

The four common ways to get a website

1. Build it yourself with a website builder

Builders such as Squarespace, Wix, or Shopify let you start with a template. The software usually costs a monthly fee, and the real price is your time: writing the text, choosing photos, setting up forms, and learning how everything works. This can suit a business on a very tight budget, as long as you are comfortable with design and willing to spend several evenings on it.

2. Hire a freelancer

Freelancers range from beginners to experienced designers. Prices vary widely—often from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars for a small site. The quality and reliability vary just as much, so look at real examples of their work and agree in writing on what is included.

3. Work with a small studio

Small studios often offer fixed-price packages for small businesses: a set number of pages, a clear timeline, and a defined list of what is included. You get a professional result without paying for a large team, and you usually know the full price before you start.

4. Hire a full-service agency

Agencies bring larger teams for strategy, design, writing, and development. That suits complex projects—online shops, custom systems, large content sites—and the price reflects it, often running into five figures. For a local or service business that needs a clear four- or five-page website, it is usually more than necessary.

What the price should include

Whichever option you choose, a professional small business website should normally include:

  • A design that works on phones. Many of your visitors will find you on a phone first.
  • Your core pages. Usually Home, About, Services, and Contact.
  • A contact form or booking path that actually delivers messages to you.
  • Basic setup for Google: page titles, descriptions, headings, and a sitemap, so search engines can understand the site.
  • Help going live on your own domain name.
  • A handoff so you own the website, the domain, and the logins.

Ongoing costs to budget for

The build is only part of the cost. Plan for these as well:

  • Domain name—your web address, renewed every year.
  • Hosting or builder subscription—the monthly fee that keeps the site online.
  • Business email—addresses like you@yourcompany.com, usually billed per user each month.
  • Updates—new prices, photos, or services over time, done by you or paid for as needed.

Hidden costs to ask about

Low prices sometimes leave important things out. Before you agree, ask whether these are included:

  • How many rounds of changes you get
  • Extra pages beyond the package, and what each costs
  • Writing the text, or whether you must supply it
  • Stock photos, fonts, or paid plugins
  • Who owns the website and domain after launch

So what is a fair price?

For most local and service businesses, a clear, professional website with four or five pages does not need an agency budget. A fair price covers a design that fits your business, a site that works on phones, a working contact path, basic Google setup, and a full handoff—with no surprises later.

The cheapest option is not always the least expensive. A website that looks dated or makes it hard to get in touch can quietly cost you customers every month.

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

  • Can I see websites you have built for businesses like mine?
  • What exactly is included, and what costs extra?
  • How long will it take, and what do you need from me?
  • Will I own the website, the domain, and all the logins?
  • What happens if I need changes after launch?
