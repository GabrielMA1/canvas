Problems addressed

Replace a vague sense of “we should improve this” with a defensible next step.

An audit is useful when the business needs better evidence and sequence before choosing a platform, vendor, redesign, campaign, or automation.

Every issue feels equally urgent.

The team has a long list of requests but no shared view of impact, effort, dependency, or order.

The proposed solution came first.

A redesign, platform, or AI tool is being considered before the underlying customer or operational problem is clear.

Internal context is difficult to challenge.

The team needs an outside review that connects observable evidence to workable options without inventing certainty.

What you receive

Findings organized for action—not a list of disconnected opinions.

The deliverables reflect the approved scope, available evidence, and the decision the audit is meant to support.

Evidence

Findings with context

Observed issues, supporting evidence, affected journeys or workflows, and relevant limitations.

Priority

Impact and effort view

A clear order for immediate fixes, deeper work, dependencies, and items that do not deserve attention yet.

Direction

Phased roadmap

Recommended phases, decision points, resource or budget considerations, and required inputs.

Action

Near-term plan

Immediate actions and, where appropriate, a 30/60/90-day plan tied to the findings and available resources.

Delivery process

Trace recommendations back to the business problem.

Timing and depth depend on scope, access, source material, analytics, the number of systems, and the decision the review should support. Review milestones are confirmed before work begins.

01 · Frame

Define the decision.

Clarify what the business is considering, why the decision matters, who owns it, and what evidence is available.

02 · Gather

Review the current state.

Collect relevant pages, materials, analytics, workflows, tools, access, constraints, customer context, and prior decisions.

03 · Evaluate

Identify patterns and causes.

Assess the evidence against the business goal, customer path, operational reality, maintainability, and known dependencies.

04 · Prioritize

Separate now, next, and later.

Organize findings by impact, effort, risk, dependency, and the team’s ability to act.

05 · Roadmap

Document the useful sequence.

Deliver findings, immediate fixes, phased recommendations, ownership considerations, and open questions.

Standards

Make the recommendation proportional to the evidence.

An audit should improve the quality of the decision, including when the right answer is to preserve or simplify what already exists.

Evidence-linked findings

Recommendations distinguish observable problems, reasonable inferences, assumptions, and questions that need confirmation.

Business-first priority

Severity is considered in the context of customer value, operational impact, risk, and current goals.

Impact and effort together

A worthwhile improvement is evaluated alongside dependencies, cost, ownership, and the team’s ability to maintain it.

Platform-neutral reasoning

Platform selection is based on content, integrations, ownership, budget, and maintenance needs.

No unsupported guarantees

Recommendations do not turn limited evidence into promises about rankings, revenue, conversions, or delivery outcomes.

Usable handoff

The findings, priorities, dependencies, decisions, and next actions are organized for the people responsible.

Fit

Use advisory work when the next decision matters more than adding output.

The review should answer a real question and have a clear owner who can act on the result.

A good fit when

  • The team needs priorities before committing budget
  • Several possible problems or solutions overlap
  • An existing site or system may be worth improving
  • Stakeholders need a shared evidence-based roadmap

Not the best fit when

  • The decision has already been made and evidence will not be considered
  • Required material, access, or ownership is unavailable
  • The expected outcome is a guaranteed result
  • The request is for a thin document without enough context to be useful
