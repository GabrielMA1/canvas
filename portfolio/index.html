Work and solution models

Work built around real business constraints.

Some client work cannot be published because of confidentiality. The examples on this page show the structure, deliverables, and decision-making used in common engagements. Items labelled “Representative solution model” are not presented as client case studies.

Representative models

See how a constraint becomes a usable system.

Each model starts with a common business situation and shows the connected work that may follow. Scope, priorities, and deliverables are confirmed for the actual business before production begins.

Representative solution model Brand

Positioning and identity foundation

For a credible business whose message and presentation no longer reflect the quality or direction of the work.

Typical decision path

  • Review the audience, offer, alternatives, and existing brand material
  • Clarify positioning, value proposition, and messaging hierarchy
  • Build a practical visual direction and identity system
  • Document usage so future touchpoints remain consistent

May include: discovery · positioning · messaging · visual identity · brand guidance

Representative solution model Web

Professional-services website and inquiry journey

A trust-focused website that clarifies the service, answers high-intent questions, and creates a direct path from research to a qualified inquiry.

Typical decision path

  • Map what prospects need to understand before contacting the business
  • Organize pages around services, fit, proof standards, and next steps
  • Design and build responsive, accessible interaction patterns
  • Connect forms, analytics, metadata, and launch QA

May include: positioning · information architecture · responsive website · forms · analytics

Representative solution model AI

AI lead qualification and routing

A practical workflow that answers common questions, captures structured details, routes the lead, and sends exceptions to a person.

Typical decision path

  • Map the questions, lead fields, rules, exceptions, and owners
  • Define the approved knowledge source and human-review boundaries
  • Build routing, notifications, integrations, and error handling
  • Validate the experience and document monitoring responsibilities

May include: conversation map · knowledge source · workflow · alerts · human handoff

Representative solution model Connected system

Local-service growth system

A connected experience combining location relevance, service pages, lead capture, follow-up, reporting, and ongoing content improvements.

Typical decision path

  • Review service demand, location relevance, customer questions, and lead flow
  • Plan the service-page structure and conversion path
  • Connect inquiry details to routing, follow-up, and reporting
  • Define ongoing content, performance, and workflow review

May include: local SEO foundations · service pages · lead routing · reporting · ongoing care

How the work is documented

Useful examples separate decisions from unsupported claims.

When an approved client case study can be published, it should explain the business situation, constraints, RielArt’s role, scope, process, deliverables, and verified evidence. Results require a clear measurement period and permission.

Context before output

The challenge, audience, workflow, constraints, and reason the work mattered provide the frame.

Scope and role

The record distinguishes what RielArt handled, what the client supplied, and which dependencies affected delivery.

Evidence before claims

Named results, quotes, and supporting material are published only with permission and enough context to be meaningful.
